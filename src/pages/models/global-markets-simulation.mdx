      <h2>Analytical Question</h2>

      <p>
        How do inventory control, execution choice, client flow and dynamic
        exposure management translate into absolute and risk-adjusted
        performance across repeated sell-side and buy-side market simulations?
      </p>

      <h2>Repeated-Session Design</h2>

      <p>
        The project draws on three timed AmplifyME Finance Accelerator – Global
        Markets sessions completed during July 2026. Each session had its own
        cohort and market path, so the comparison is descriptive rather than a
        controlled experiment.
      </p>

      <p>
        The strongest buy-side evidence comes from 22 July, when the
        asset-management portfolio ranked fourth by P&amp;L. The strongest
        sell-side evidence comes from 27 July, when the dealer-trading result
        ranked 8th of 93. These outcomes did not occur during the same event and
        are dated explicitly throughout the page. The 15 July buy-side session
        and 22 July sell-side session are retained as process baselines.
      </p>

      <p>
        In the sell-side role, the mandate was to facilitate client flow,
        generate commission revenue and trading P&amp;L, use the exchange when
        appropriate and manage dealer inventory without avoidable market impact
        or operational errors. On the buy side, capital had to be allocated,
        trades routed through brokers and the exchange, and exposure adjusted as
        the market evolved.
      </p>

      <h2>Principal Buy-Side Result — 22 July</h2>

      <p>
        The portfolio generated <strong>$2,907,448 of total P&amp;L</strong>,
        equivalent to a <strong>14.5% return on initial funds</strong>, and
        finished with a value of <strong>$22,690,856.40</strong>. This ranked
        fourth by asset-management P&amp;L. The denominator is deliberately left
        unpublished because the portal's 153-participant event count and a
        manual count of 148 ranked participants have not been reconciled.
      </p>

      <p>
        Absolute performance was stronger than the broader platform score. The
        exact composite score was 79.18%, ranking 25th of 153, while the Sharpe
        ratio was 0.66. The distinction matters: the session produced strong raw
        financial outcomes, but risk-adjusted performance and several
        proprietary platform measures were less striking than the P&amp;L rank.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="buy-side" />

      <h2>Exposure Management</h2>

      <p>
        The portfolio was concentrated in equities for much of the 22 July
        session. Average allocation was 55.9% equities and approximately 44.1%
        cash, while equity exposure rose to approximately 90–94% at its highest
        point. Risk was subsequently reduced materially, with the portfolio
        ending at 26.4% equities and 73.6% cash.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsAllocation />

      <p>
        This supports a narrow conclusion: the portfolio retained sufficient
        equity exposure during the principal return-generating phase and then
        de-risked before the close. It does not establish whether every exposure
        change was optimally timed because the transaction-level history and
        exact intraday allocation series are unavailable.
      </p>

      <h2>Principal Sell-Side Result — 27 July</h2>

      <p>
        The 27 July dealer-trading session ranked <strong>8th of 93 on the
        sell-side leaderboard</strong> with an <strong>88.89% sell-side composite
        score</strong>. It generated <strong>$842,052 of trading P&amp;L</strong>
        and <strong>$153,635 of commission revenue</strong>, producing
        <strong>$995,687 of total simulated P&amp;L</strong>.
      </p>

      <p>
        The session completed 499 exchange trades and 13 chat trades. The
        exchange-trade and fat-finger-control metrics were both 100%, with no
        recorded exchange or client fat-finger incidents and 0% recorded average
        exchange impact. The P&amp;L display metric was 98%, while SSRM was
        80.95%.
      </p>

      <p>
        Average net exposure was -$10,410,039 and average contract exposure was
        -37,152. Those figures were materially larger in absolute magnitude than
        on 22 July. The stronger financial output and platform risk-management
        score therefore do not justify claiming that every improvement reflected
        lower dealer risk.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="sell-side" />

      <h2>Sell-Side Improvement — 22 Versus 27 July</h2>

      <p>
        The second sell-side session increased the composite score by 8.54
        percentage points, commission revenue by 53.0%, trading P&amp;L by 24.3%
        and total P&amp;L by 28.0%. Exchange activity increased by 27.3%, while
        the commission metric and SSRM rose by 11.12 and 18.26 percentage points
        respectively.
      </p>

      <p>
        The ordinal rank improved from 13th of 153 to 8th of 93. Relative
        percentile performance was broadly stable at approximately the top
        8.5–8.6% of each cohort, supporting the more measured conclusion of two
        consecutive top-decile sell-side finishes.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsSellSideComparison />

      <h2>Buy-Side Progression — 15 Versus 22 July</h2>

      <p>
        The 15 July asset-management session provides an earlier process
        baseline rather than a second flagship result. One week later, the
        composite score, ending portfolio value, BSRM score and operational
        accuracy were all stronger. The comparison remains descriptive because
        the sessions had different cohorts, market paths and P&amp;L field
        definitions.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsSessionComparison />

      <h2>Performance Versus Platform Score</h2>

      <p>
        The portal separates raw financial outcomes from composite scores built
        from proprietary subscores. On 22 July, asset-management P&amp;L ranked
        fourth while the composite ranking was 25th of 153. ROI and BSRM were
        strong, while ES and RA were materially lower. On 27 July, the
        sell-side composite score was 88.89%, with the strongest displayed measures in exchange
        trading, operational control and P&amp;L.
      </p>

      <p>
        The results suggest that absolute output, risk-adjusted performance and
        platform scoring should be interpreted separately. They do not prove
        that any single subscore caused the composite ranking because AmplifyME's weighting formula is unavailable and complete acronym
        definitions are not provided.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="scores" />

      <h2>What Worked</h2>

      <ul>
        <li>
          Meaningful equity exposure captured the principal buy-side
          return-generating phase on 22 July.
        </li>
        <li>
          Exposure was reduced materially before the close, leaving 73.6% of the
          final portfolio in cash.
        </li>
        <li>
          The strongest buy-side session ranked fourth by P&amp;L after generating
          $2.91 million and a 14.5% return.
        </li>
        <li>
          The 27 July sell-side session increased commission generation, trading
          P&amp;L and total P&amp;L relative to the 22 July baseline.
        </li>
        <li>
          Both principal sell-side sessions finished in the top decile of their
          respective cohorts.
        </li>
        <li>
          High exchange activity was completed without recorded fat-finger
          incidents and with 0% recorded average exchange impact.
        </li>
      </ul>

      <h2>What Could Be Improved</h2>

      <ul>
        <li>
          Risk-adjusted buy-side consistency could improve beyond the 0.66
          Sharpe ratio despite the strong absolute P&amp;L result.
        </li>
        <li>
          The buy-side result relied on concentrated directional equity
          exposure. Formal concentration limits and staged de-risking rules
          would make the process more repeatable.
        </li>
        <li>
          The lower ES and RA portal scores warrant review, although their exact
          definitions and weights should not be inferred.
        </li>
        <li>
          Average dealer exposure was materially larger on 27 July despite the
          stronger SSRM score. Clearer inventory limits would help distinguish
          client-facilitation returns from directional dealer risk.
        </li>
        <li>
          Commission generation improved substantially but remained an area for
          continued process refinement relative to the highest-ranked dealers.
        </li>
      </ul>
