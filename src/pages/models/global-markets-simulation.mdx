      <h2>Analytical Question</h2>

      <p>
        How do inventory control, execution choice, client flow and dynamic
        exposure management translate into absolute and risk-adjusted
        performance across sell-side and buy-side roles?
      </p>

      <p>
        The exercise was completed through AmplifyME's Finance Accelerator –
        Global Markets programme on 22 July 2026. It was a timed, live,
        multi-participant market simulation rather than an M&amp;A or corporate-
        finance investment-banking case.
      </p>

      <h2>Simulation Design</h2>

      <p>
        The first session placed me in an investment-bank trading role. The
        objective was to facilitate client flow, generate commission revenue
        and trading P&amp;L, use the exchange when appropriate and manage the
        resulting dealer inventory without creating avoidable market impact or
        operational errors.
      </p>

      <p>
        The second session moved to the buy side. Capital had to be allocated,
        trades routed through brokers and the exchange, and portfolio exposure
        adjusted as the market evolved. The two roles were connected by the
        same underlying tension: taking enough risk to generate returns while
        controlling execution cost, concentration and inventory.
      </p>

      <h2>Asset-Management Performance</h2>

      <p>
        The portfolio generated <strong>$2,907,448 of total P&amp;L</strong>,
        equivalent to a <strong>14.5% return on initial funds</strong>, and
        finished with a value of <strong>$22,690,856.40</strong>. This ranked
        fourth by asset-management P&amp;L. The denominator is deliberately left
        unpublished because the portal's 153-participant event count and a
        manual count of 148 ranked participants have not been reconciled.
      </p>

      <p>
        Absolute performance was stronger than the broader platform score. The
        exact composite score was 79.18%, ranking 25th of 153, while the Sharpe
        ratio was 0.66. The divergence matters: the session produced strong raw
        financial outcomes, but the result should not be presented as uniformly
        dominant across risk-adjusted, execution and proprietary scoring
        measures.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="buy-side" />

      <h2>Exposure Management</h2>

      <p>
        The portfolio was concentrated in equities for much of the session.
        Average allocation was 55.9% equities and approximately 44.1% cash,
        while equity exposure rose to approximately 90–94% at its highest
        point. Risk was subsequently reduced materially, with the portfolio
        ending at 26.4% equities and 73.6% cash.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsAllocation />

      <p>
        This supports a narrow conclusion: the portfolio maintained sufficient
        equity exposure to capture the principal return-generating period and
        then de-risked. It does not establish whether every exposure change was
        optimally timed because the transaction-level history and exact
        intraday allocation series are not available.
      </p>

      <h2>Sell-Side Performance</h2>

      <p>
        The dealer role generated <strong>$677,563 of trading P&amp;L</strong>
        and <strong>$100,405 of commission revenue</strong>, producing
        <strong>$777,968 of total P&amp;L</strong>. The result ranked 13th of
        153 on the sell-side leaderboard.
      </p>

      <p>
        Execution volume was materially higher than on the buy side: 392
        exchange trades and 11 chat trades were completed. The exchange-trade
        and fat-finger-control metrics were both 100%, with no recorded exchange
        or client fat-finger incidents and 0% recorded average exchange impact.
        Average net exposure was negative at -$1,634,953; without the complete
        market path and scoring methodology, that sign is not interpreted as
        inherently favourable or unfavourable.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="sell-side" />

      <h2>Performance Versus Platform Score</h2>

      <p>
        The portal separates raw financial outcomes from a composite score built
        from proprietary subscores. On the buy side, P&amp;L ranked fourth while
        the composite ranking was 25th of 153. ROI and BSRM were strong, while
        ES and RA were materially lower. On the sell side, the exchange-trade
        and operational-control measures were strongest, while commission and
        sell-side-risk-management scores were lower.
      </p>

      <p>
        The results are consistent with strong absolute returns accompanied by
        concentration and less exceptional risk-adjusted or execution-related
        scoring. They do not prove that any single subscore caused the composite
        ranking because AmplifyME's weighting formula is unavailable.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="scores" />

      <h2>Progress Across Repeated Sessions</h2>

      <p>
        An earlier asset-management session on 15 July provides a useful
        process baseline rather than a second flagship result. One week later,
        the composite score, ending portfolio value, BSRM score and operational
        accuracy were all stronger. The comparison remains descriptive because
        the two sessions had different cohorts, market paths and P&amp;L field
        definitions.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsSessionComparison />

      <h2>What Worked</h2>

      <ul>
        <li>
          Maintaining meaningful equity exposure during the main return-
          generating phase supported strong absolute P&amp;L.
        </li>
        <li>
          Exposure was reduced materially later in the session, leaving the
          portfolio with 73.6% cash rather than carrying peak risk into the
          close.
        </li>
        <li>
          Sell-side execution combined trading P&amp;L with commission income
          rather than relying on only one revenue source.
        </li>
        <li>
          High exchange activity was completed without recorded fat-finger
          incidents and with 0% recorded average exchange impact.
        </li>
      </ul>

      <h2>What Could Be Improved</h2>

      <ul>
        <li>
          Risk-adjusted consistency could be improved; a 0.66 Sharpe ratio was
          respectable but materially less striking than the absolute P&amp;L rank.
        </li>
        <li>
          The buy-side result relied on concentrated directional equity
          exposure. Clearer pre-trade concentration limits and staged de-risking
          rules would make the process more repeatable.
        </li>
        <li>
          The lower ES and RA portal scores indicate areas for review, although
          their exact definitions and weights should not be inferred.
        </li>
        <li>
          On the sell side, stronger commission generation and more disciplined
          inventory limits could improve the lower commission and SSRM
          subscores.
        </li>
      </ul>
