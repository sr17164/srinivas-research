      <h2>Analytical Question</h2>

      <p>
        How do inventory control, execution choice, client flow and dynamic
        exposure management translate into absolute and risk-adjusted outcomes
        across repeated buy-side and sell-side market simulations?
      </p>

      <h2>Evidence Design</h2>

      <p>
        The project uses timed AmplifyME Finance Accelerator – Global Markets
        sessions completed on 15, 22, 27 and 29 July and 3 August 2026. Each
        session had a different cohort, market path and news sequence. Comparisons
        are therefore descriptive rather than controlled, and platform scores are
        reported separately from financial outcomes.
      </p>

      <p>
        The strongest role-specific results occurred on different dates. The
        principal buy-side evidence comes from 29 July; the principal sell-side
        evidence comes from 27 July. The 3 August event is retained because it
        provides the strongest same-event evidence across both mandates, not
        because it supersedes either flagship result.
      </p>

      <h2>Principal Buy-Side Result — 29 July</h2>

      <p>
        The asset-management session scored <strong>93.05%</strong> and appeared
        <strong>2nd of 102 on the displayed leaderboard</strong>. Event
        administration subsequently voided the displayed first-place result,
        leaving this as the <strong>highest valid score</strong>. The displayed
        rank and post-event status are both retained; the result is not described
        as an unqualified first place.
      </p>

      <p>
        The portal reported <strong>$3,801,651 of P&amp;L</strong>, a
        <strong>19.0% return</strong> and a <strong>1.20 Sharpe ratio</strong>.
        Its 100% ROI field is a proprietary subscore, not a 100% investment
        return. The detailed and leaderboard screens also use different labels
        for the $3.80m field, and the displayed portfolio value does not
        mechanically reconcile with P&amp;L and commission.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="buy-side" />

      <h2>Allocation and Risk Interpretation</h2>

      <p>
        Average allocation was 70.2% equities and 29.8% cash; the portfolio
        finished 99.5% in equities. That exposure captured the main
        return-generating phase, but it also left the result dependent on a highly
        concentrated final allocation. No transaction timestamps or
        security-level weights are reconstructed from the portal chart.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsAllocation />

      <h2>Principal Sell-Side Result — 27 July</h2>

      <p>
        The dealer-trading session ranked <strong>8th of 93</strong> with an
        <strong>88.89% composite score</strong>. It produced
        <strong>$842,052 of trading P&amp;L</strong> and
        <strong>$153,635 of commission</strong>, for
        <strong>$995,687 of portal-reported total simulated P&amp;L</strong>.
        No exchange or client fat-finger incident was recorded.
      </p>

      <p>
        Average net exposure was -$10,410,039 and average contract exposure was
        -37,152. Stronger financial and platform outcomes therefore do not imply
        that dealer inventory risk fell on every measure.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="sell-side" />

      <h2>Same-Event Cross-Role Result — 3 August</h2>

      <p>
        The asset-management phase ranked <strong>5th of 93</strong> with an
        <strong>89.68% score</strong> and a
        <strong>$3,330,183 platform trading-P&amp;L field</strong>. The sell-side
        phase ranked <strong>9th of 96</strong> with an
        <strong>89.04% score</strong>, $893,072 of trading P&amp;L and $202,035
        of commission.
      </p>

      <p>
        The sell-side total of <strong>$1,095,107 is derived</strong> by adding
        trading P&amp;L and commission because the portal did not show a total
        field. The significance of the event is simultaneous top-decile
        performance across both roles; it is not presented as another flagship.
      </p>

      <h2>Session Record and Failure Evidence</h2>

      <p>
        The ledger preserves both stronger and weaker sessions. In particular,
        the 29 July sell-side phase generated $192,145 of commission but a
        $496,309 trading loss, producing total simulated P&amp;L of -$304,164.
        This is useful evidence that client-process and operational metrics can
        remain credible while dealer inventory produces a poor financial result.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsSessionLedger />

      <h2>Measured Lessons</h2>

      <ul>
        <li>
          Buy-side return, Sharpe ratio, execution score and composite score all
          improved between the 22 and 29 July principal sessions, but final
          concentration also increased materially.
        </li>
        <li>
          Sell-side commission, trading P&amp;L and composite score improved in
          the principal 27 July session, while average dealer exposure remained
          substantial.
        </li>
        <li>
          A formal late-session de-risking rule would make the buy-side process
          less dependent on remaining almost fully invested into the close.
        </li>
        <li>
          Client facilitation, commission generation and speculative dealer
          inventory should be monitored as separate sources of outcome.
        </li>
        <li>
          Proprietary platform subscores cannot be treated as causes of ranking
          or financial performance without the weighting formula.
        </li>
      </ul>
