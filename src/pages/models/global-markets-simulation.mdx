      <h2>Analytical Question</h2>

      <p>
        How do inventory control, execution choice, client flow and dynamic
        exposure management translate into absolute and risk-adjusted
        performance across repeated sell-side and buy-side market simulations?
      </p>

      <h2>Repeated-Session Design</h2>

      <p>
        The project draws on timed AmplifyME Finance Accelerator – Global
        Markets sessions completed on 15, 22, 27 and 29 July 2026. Each session
        had its own cohort, market path and news sequence, so the comparisons are
        descriptive rather than controlled experiments.
      </p>

      <p>
        The principal buy-side evidence now comes from 29 July: a 93.05%
        asset-management composite score, $3,801,651 of portal-reported
        P&amp;L, a 19.0% return and a 1.20 Sharpe ratio. The principal sell-side
        evidence remains the 27 July dealer-trading session, which ranked 8th of
        93 with an 88.89% sell-side composite score. These strongest role-specific
        results came from different dates and are treated separately throughout.
      </p>

      <p>
        In the sell-side role, the mandate was to facilitate client flow,
        generate commission revenue and trading P&amp;L, use the exchange when
        appropriate and manage dealer inventory without avoidable market impact
        or operational errors. On the buy side, capital had to be allocated,
        trades routed through brokers and the exchange, and exposure adjusted as
        the market evolved.
      </p>

      <h2>Principal Buy-Side Result — 29 July</h2>

      <p>
        The asset-management session scored <strong>93.05%</strong> and placed
        <strong>2nd of 102 on the displayed leaderboard</strong>. Event
        administration subsequently voided the displayed first-place result,
        leaving this as the <strong>highest valid score</strong>. The portal has
        retained its original ordering, so the displayed rank is reported
        alongside the post-event status rather than being converted into an
        unqualified first-place claim.
      </p>

      <p>
        The platform reported <strong>$3,801,651 of P&amp;L</strong>, a
        <strong>19.0% return on initial funds</strong> and a
        <strong>1.20 Sharpe ratio</strong>. The exact execution subscore was
        96.08%, BSRM was 98.98%, the RA subscore was 70.19% and the ROI platform
        subscore was 100%. The 100% value is a proprietary subscore—not a 100%
        portfolio return. Seven chat-and-voice trades and five exchange trades
        were completed with no recorded fat-finger incident.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="buy-side" />

      <h2>Allocation and Risk Path</h2>

      <p>
        The session began with most capital held in cash. Equity exposure was
        increased materially, fluctuated through several stages and subsequently
        rose to approximately 100%. Average allocation was 70.2% equities and
        29.8% cash; the portfolio finished at 99.5% equities and 0.5% cash.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsAllocation />

      <p>
        Substantial equity exposure captured the principal return-generating
        phase, but the position was not materially de-risked before the close.
        The strong return, Sharpe ratio and platform risk-management subscore
        therefore need to be interpreted alongside a highly concentrated final
        allocation. No exact transaction timestamps or security-level weights
        are reconstructed from the portal chart.
      </p>

      <h2>Buy-Side Progression — 22 Versus 29 July</h2>

      <p>
        Relative to the prior principal buy-side session, portal-reported P&amp;L
        increased by $894,203, or 30.8%. Return increased by 4.5 percentage
        points, the Sharpe ratio increased by 0.54 and the composite score rose by
        13.87 percentage points. Execution scoring improved especially sharply,
        while commission expenditure declined modestly.
      </p>

      <p>
        The allocation evidence prevents a one-sided interpretation. The 22 July
        portfolio finished 73.6% in cash after substantial de-risking; the 29 July
        portfolio finished 99.5% invested in equities. Stronger financial and
        platform outcomes were therefore accompanied by materially greater final
        concentration.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsSessionComparison />

      <h2>Principal Sell-Side Result — 27 July</h2>

      <p>
        The 27 July dealer-trading session ranked
        <strong>8th of 93 on the sell-side leaderboard</strong> with an
        <strong>88.89% sell-side composite score</strong>. It generated
        <strong>$842,052 of trading P&amp;L</strong> and
        <strong>$153,635 of commission revenue</strong>, producing
        <strong>$995,687 of total simulated P&amp;L</strong>.
      </p>

      <p>
        The session completed 499 exchange trades and 13 chat trades. The
        exchange-trade and fat-finger-control metrics were both 100%, with no
        recorded exchange or client fat-finger incidents and 0% recorded average
        exchange impact. The P&amp;L display metric was 98%, while SSRM was
        80.95%.
      </p>

      <p>
        Average net exposure was -$10,410,039 and average contract exposure was
        -37,152. Those figures were materially larger in absolute magnitude than
        on 22 July. The stronger financial output and platform risk-management
        score therefore do not justify claiming that every improvement reflected
        lower dealer risk.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="sell-side" />

      <h2>Sell-Side Improvement — 22 Versus 27 July</h2>

      <p>
        The second sell-side session increased the composite score by 8.54
        percentage points, commission revenue by 53.0%, trading P&amp;L by 24.3%
        and total P&amp;L by 28.0%. Exchange activity increased by 27.3%, while
        the commission metric and SSRM rose by 11.12 and 18.26 percentage points
        respectively.
      </p>

      <p>
        The ordinal rank improved from 13th of 153 to 8th of 93. Relative
        percentile performance was broadly stable at approximately the top
        8.5–8.6% of each cohort, supporting the measured conclusion of two
        consecutive top-decile sell-side finishes.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsSellSideComparison />

      <h2>Other Sessions</h2>

      <p>
        The complete ledger preserves both stronger and weaker sessions. In the
        29 July sell-side phase, commission revenue reached $192,145 and
        operational metrics remained reasonably strong, but a $496,309 trading
        loss produced total P&amp;L of -$304,164. This separates
        execution and client-process quality from the directional outcome of the
        dealer inventory.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsSessionLedger />

      <h2>Performance Versus Platform Score</h2>

      <p>
        Financial results and platform scores measure different things. The 29
        July asset-management session combined stronger absolute P&amp;L, return
        and Sharpe ratio with materially improved execution and BSRM subscores.
        However, the final 99.5% equity allocation remains relevant despite the
        98.98% BSRM platform subscore. A high proprietary subscore is not
        synonymous with low real-world portfolio risk.
      </p>

      <p>
        The 29 July sell-side phase provides the reverse example: commission,
        execution and operational-control measures remained credible, yet
        directional dealer P&amp;L was negative. The weighting formula is
        unavailable, so no single subscore is treated as the definitive cause of
        a composite ranking or financial outcome.
      </p>

      <GlobalMarketsMetrics mode="scores" />

      <h2>What Worked</h2>

      <ul>
        <li>
          Portal-reported buy-side P&amp;L increased by $894,203 from the prior
          principal session, while return rose from 14.5% to 19.0%.
        </li>
        <li>
          The Sharpe ratio improved from 0.66 to 1.20 and the composite score
          increased by 13.87 percentage points.
        </li>
        <li>
          Execution scoring increased by 48.78 percentage points, BSRM reached
          98.98% and no fat-finger incident was recorded.
        </li>
        <li>
          Sufficient equity exposure was maintained to capture the main
          return-generating phase of the 29 July buy-side session.
        </li>
        <li>
          The result became the highest valid asset-management score after the
          displayed first-place entry was voided, while retaining the displayed
          2nd-of-102 ranking for transparency.
        </li>
        <li>
          The principal sell-side session remained a separate 8th-of-93 result,
          supported by stronger commission generation, P&amp;L and repeatable
          operational control.
        </li>
      </ul>

      <h2>What Could Be Improved</h2>

      <ul>
        <li>
          The 99.5% final equity allocation was highly concentrated. A formal
          late-session de-risking rule would make the process less dependent on
          remaining fully invested into the close.
        </li>
        <li>
          The RA platform subscore of 70.19% remained materially below the other
          principal buy-side subscores.
        </li>
        <li>
          The improved return and Sharpe ratio should be preserved while reducing
          dependence on a near-fully invested final portfolio.
        </li>
        <li>
          Portal P&amp;L and portfolio-value fields require clearer reconciliation
          where more complete source data becomes available.
        </li>
        <li>
          The 29 July sell-side phase needs a better separation between client
          facilitation, commission generation and speculative inventory risk.
        </li>
        <li>
          Average dealer exposure remained substantial in the principal 27 July
          sell-side session despite the stronger SSRM score.
        </li>
      </ul>
