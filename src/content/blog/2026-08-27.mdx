## Executive Summary

<ResearchPanel label="Current view" title="Structural support, limited tactical asymmetry">

I am neutral gold over the next three to six months. The balance of evidence has improved since the June liquidation: central-bank demand rebounded to **289 tonnes in Q2**, global gold-backed exchange-traded funds returned to net inflows in July, and Chinese official and ETF demand strengthened into August.

The opportunity cost remains restrictive. The US Treasury's 10-year real yield was **2.34% on 26 August**, still above the 2.15% level that invalidated my earlier 2026 bullish thesis. The World Gold Council described the August move towards **$4,400/oz** as more tactical than broad-based, with investment flows improving but seasonal demand in China and India still quiet.

That combination does not justify a fresh short: official buying and reserve diversification provide a durable bid. It also does not justify chasing the rebound while real yields remain high and investor demand is capable of reversing abruptly. I would require either a sustained fall in real yields with broader ETF participation, or a material price reset with the structural demand evidence intact, before adopting a bullish stance.

## What Changed Since July

This is a new view, not a continuation of the unsuccessful January and February gold calls. Those trades had explicit price targets and were invalidated when real yields rose beyond their stated thresholds. The present question is whether the subsequent improvement in physical and investment demand is strong enough to offset that higher-yield regime.

Three pieces of evidence shifted positively.

First, the World Gold Council revised Q1 central-bank demand down to **56.5 tonnes**, then estimated **288.9 tonnes** of net buying in Q2. The recovery was unusually strong, but the weaker first quarter left first-half demand at **345 tonnes**, the lowest first-half total since 2022. The correct conclusion is therefore that official demand recovered, not that it was uniformly strong throughout 2026.

Second, global gold-backed ETFs added approximately **$3bn and 23 tonnes in July**, reversing two months of outflows. Chinese ETFs added 5 tonnes during July and approximately another 8 tonnes through 12 August. These flows show that investor demand can return quickly, but the first-half record also shows how concentrated liquidation episodes can overwhelm the physical bid.

Third, the People's Bank of China reported a **20-tonne** increase in July, taking its disclosed holdings to **2,366 tonnes**. The purchase extended the reported accumulation streak to 21 months. This strengthens the reserve-diversification argument, while leaving open how much buying would persist at materially higher prices.

## Why the View Is Not Bullish Yet

### Real Yields Remain Above the Prior Invalidation Level

The 10-year US real yield was 2.34% on 26 August. That is slightly below 2.40% on 21 August, but still well above the 2.15% threshold used in the failed February thesis.

Gold can rise alongside high real yields when geopolitical demand, fiscal uncertainty or official purchases dominate. The relationship is not mechanical. However, a high real yield raises the cost of holding a non-yielding asset and makes Western ETF demand more sensitive to monetary-policy expectations and the dollar.

### The August Rebound Is Still Flow-Sensitive

World Gold Council analysis published on 20 August linked the move towards $4,400/oz to softer US data, a pickup in ETF flows and continued official buying, while characterising the rally as more tactical than broad-based. That distinction matters. A rally led by a narrow change in rates expectations can reverse if inflation, policy or the dollar reprices.

### High Prices Constrain Some Physical Demand

Q2 jewellery consumption fell 17% year on year to 278 tonnes. Bar and coin demand was steadier at 307 tonnes, but remained well below Q1. Structural demand therefore coexists with price elasticity in consumer channels; it should not be described as a universal price floor.

## Decision Framework

<ResearchPanel label="Upgrade conditions" title="What would turn the view bullish">

- A sustained decline in the US 10-year real yield below approximately 2.10%
- Broad global ETF inflows that persist beyond a short rates-driven episode
- Continued official purchases without a renewed rise in disclosed selling
- A weaker US dollar alongside stable inflation expectations
- A material price correction that leaves the official-demand thesis intact

</ResearchPanel>

<ResearchPanel label="Downside conditions" title="What would turn the view bearish">

- A sustained 10-year real yield above 2.50% with a stronger dollar
- Renewed, broad ETF outflows rather than isolated month-end liquidation
- A visible slowdown in reported central-bank purchases
- Further weakness in Asian investment demand and physical premiums
- A break in the August rebound accompanied by deteriorating flows

</ResearchPanel>

The probabilities are judgemental and describe the relative balance of evidence rather than a statistical forecast.

## Conclusion

Gold's structural case remains credible, but structural demand and tactical price validity are different questions. Q2 official buying, July ETF inflows and renewed Chinese accumulation reduce the case for a short. A 2.34% real yield, weak jewellery volumes and the still-tactical character of the August rebound reduce the case for a fresh long.

The appropriate stance is neutral over three to six months. The view would become bullish only if the opportunity cost falls or investor participation broadens without relying on another short-lived policy repricing.
